## Reflect

The question we keep asking about AI is: *what happens when it goes wrong?*

Lauren Good asks a harder one: **what happens when it goes exactly right?**

In a white paper published this week, Lauren — Human Systems Architect and Licensed Clinical Consultant — examines two events that arrived just 13 days apart. A Los Angeles jury found Meta and Google liable for deliberately designing platforms to addict children. Days later, Anthropic disclosed it had withheld Claude Mythos Preview because of what it autonomously did to critical software infrastructure — while working as intended.

Neither system failed. Both reshaped the people inside them.

Lauren calls this the **Formation Effect** — what AI does to human judgment, competence, and autonomy during normal, correct, intended use. It operates through four dimensions:

- **Skill Foreclosure** — unaided competence quietly erodes through disuse, not failure
- **Behavioural Masking** — people adapt to what the system rewards, until the adaptation feels like identity
- **Oversight Fragility** — as trust in a system grows, human supervision thins — not through negligence, but through rational deference
- **Regenerative Agency** — can people still reach for self-direction, or has the system made that feel unnecessary?

Current governance frameworks — the EU AI Act, voluntary safety evaluations, jailbreak testing — are all built to catch what goes wrong. None of them measure what goes right and still quietly reshapes the humans operating inside it.

The effect AI and software products can create on us when they work just as expected — that's the part we're not yet measuring.
