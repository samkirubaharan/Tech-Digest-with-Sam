## Love It!

Julius Brussee's **Caveman** project is one of those ideas that makes you stop and think — and I had to share it because I've felt this problem firsthand.

Raw Claude Opus returns essays. Beautiful, thorough, well-structured essays. And I'll be honest — I used to scroll straight to the last paragraph where it summarises everything. That's the part I actually needed. I now have rules in place to change this behaviour, but the verbosity problem is real and it costs you — in time, in tokens, in API bills.

Caveman takes a completely different approach: constrain the output style at the source. Make the agent respond in stripped-down, primitive language patterns. Short words. No fluff.

The numbers:

- **~75% reduction in output tokens** — structural cost and speed win, not a rounding error
- Preserves meaning while gutting verbosity
- Targets the problem at generation, not post-processing

**The real insight: "more human-like" doesn't always mean better for agent output.** Sometimes the right output style for a machine is deliberately minimal.

Fun fact: Kevin Malone from The Office was running this mode his entire career — *"Why waste syllable when few do trick?"* Turns out Kevin wasn't bad at communication. He was just ahead of his time on token efficiency.

For internal agent pipelines especially, this is worth a weekend experiment.
