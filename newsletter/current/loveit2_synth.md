## Love It!

Sam Altman's "What I Wish Someone Had Told Me" is the kind of post that keeps circulating because it earns the rereads. Rubén Domínguez Ibar surfaced the six points worth holding onto:

1. **Optimism, obsession, and raw horsepower are how things get started**
2. **Unreasonable commitment and long-term orientation are how things get finished**
3. **Audacious ideas motivate people more than easy ones**
4. **Incentives are superpowers — set them carefully**
5. **Concentrate on a small number of high-conviction bets**
6. **Communicate clearly and concisely**

The gap between points 1 and 2 is where most things die. Starting energy is available to almost everyone. The unreasonable commitment to see something through — past the point where it stops being exciting and becomes just difficult — is genuinely rare.

Point 3 is the one that surprises people when they first encounter it. The instinct is to make goals feel achievable by scaling them down. The research and practice both point the other way: ambitious targets generate more effort and more creative problem-solving than safe ones. Motivation scales with the size of the idea.

Point 5 is the operational version of that same insight. Not "do fewer things" as a productivity tip — but concentrate on a small number of bets you actually believe in, and give them the resources that belief implies.

Probably worth more than most business books. Read the original post for the full version.
