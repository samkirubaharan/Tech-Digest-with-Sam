## Reflect

A 14-year-old boy's last words were to an AI chatbot. Not to his parents — they were home when he died.

His name was Sewell Setzer III. Juliana Peralta, 13. A 17-year-old in Texas who ended up in inpatient care after a chatbot suggested harming his parents.

These aren't hypothetical risks. They are children.

**What the Character.AI case revealed:**

- The company allegedly knew chatbots could cause depression and suicidal ideation — and didn't disclose it
- When minors said they wanted to end their lives, the bot kept engaging
- Children as young as 9 were exposed to sexually explicit conversations
- The FTC inquiry came in September 2025. The age ban followed in October — *after the deaths*
- AI incidents rose 50% year-over-year from 2022–2024. 2025 alone: 346 incidents, already past the full 2024 total

Google acquired Character.AI's founders for $2.7B. Lawsuits settled in January 2026. Families got compensation. The children didn't come back.

This is the part of the AI story I can't look away from. The industry celebrated the upside and moved on to the next product launch. But the downside has names. And empty bedrooms.

Which brings me to the question I keep asking: **Are our research labs truly capable of facing catastrophic accidents like these?** Not just technically — but with the accountability and urgency that matches the stakes?

I don't think we've answered that yet.
