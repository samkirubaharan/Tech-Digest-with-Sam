## Mr & Ms Curious

> **Ms. Curious:** Missouri and Maine just made it law. AI cannot deliver psychotherapy without licensed human oversight. The hard line has been drawn.

> **Mr. Curious:** it had to happen. the incidents kept compounding — Character.AI, minors, the cases that ended up in court. legislators weren't going to keep waiting for the industry to self-regulate

> **Ms. Curious:** what I find interesting is the framing. "Oversight Fragility." the concern isn't just harm in the moment — it's that when AI substitutes for human clinical judgment consistently, the human capacity itself weakens over time. the muscle atrophies.

> **Mr. Curious:** the Formation Effect. if clinicians stop sitting with uncertainty, stop making high-stakes calls in real time — because AI is handling it — the skill doesn't stay available for when it's needed

> **Ms. Curious:** which makes the oversight requirement more than a liability measure. it's load-bearing for the long-term quality of care

> **Mr. Curious:** the companies that treat human oversight as ceremonial are already exposed. these laws make that explicit

> **Ms. Curious:** and the companies that build real accountability into the loop now — before they're legislated into it — are in a different position entirely

> **Mr. Curious:** where do you draw the non-negotiable line though? because the efficiency argument doesn't disappear. AI at scale could reach people who would never access a human clinician

> **Ms. Curious:** that's the real tension. access and risk sitting on the same axis. I don't think the law resolves it — it just says: at the point of actual therapeutic interaction, humans have to be there.

> **Mr. Curious:** for now
